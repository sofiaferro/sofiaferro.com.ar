title: a better byte
descriptor: electronic literature in three.js
summary: A homage to Charles Bukowski's Macintosh IIsi.

> I am aware that a computer can't create a poem.
> But neither can a typewriter.
>
> — Charles Bukowski, "My Computer"

[a better byte](https://a-better-byte.vercel.app/) is a homage to Charles Bukowski's Macintosh IIsi, acquired in 1990. When he moved from the typewriter to the computer, even two of his editors wrote to question it. And yet, those were his most prolific years.

The piece is his room at night, built in three.js from a real photo: the messy desk, the radio, the wine, the ashtray and the lamp. His poems about the computer are MacWrite documents in a System 6 Finder folder, and they open, read and close like on the Mac. "print" sends them through a Personal LaserWriter: for five of them, the page is the real scan of his printout, and two are signed. "the modern life", which opens with "just lost a poem on this computer", is lost to the System 6 bomb. Closing the last poem brings March 9, 1994, and the screen goes dark.

Nothing moves on its own: the viewer scrolls the poem, opens another one, prints, or grabs the lamp or the desk mouse. The Mac is modeled in code with Apple's measurements; the mouse and the printer, in Blender through the MCP; the lamp, from Poly Haven, split into pieces and articulated like an Anglepoise. The machine's sounds (the mouse, the keys, the printer) are synthesized with Web Audio, with no audio files. The shadows and the moonlight are painted images, and the room is only drawn when something changes.

The name plays on a letter to Ivan Suvanjieff dated February 20, 1992, quoted by [RealityStudio](https://realitystudio.org/bibliographic-bunker/charles-bukowski-william-burroughs-and-the-computer/): "There is something about seeing your words on a screen before you that makes you send the word with a better bite, sighted in closer to the target." If [botkowski](/en/projects/botkowski) recombined his words, this time it goes back to the machine that started it all.
